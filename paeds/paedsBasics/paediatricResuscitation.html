Paediatric Resuscitation (ABC)

Use an ABC approach with age-appropriate vital signs, weight-based dosing, and early senior help.

Weight estimation if unknown: weight (kg) ≈ (age in years + 4) × 2

A – Airway

  • Assess: talking/crying, stridor, gurgling, apnoea.
  • Position: neutral in infants, sniffing in older children.
  • Open airway: jaw thrust in trauma, chin lift otherwise.
  • Suction secretions, vomit, blood.
  • Adjuncts: oropharyngeal airway (if no gag), nasopharyngeal if semi-conscious.
  • Consider early senior/anaesthesia help in anticipated difficult airway.

B – Breathing

  • Look: work of breathing, accessory muscles, recession, cyanosis.
  • Listen: wheeze, crackles, absent breath sounds.
  • Feel: chest expansion, tracheal position.
  • Give high-flow oxygen via non-rebreather if hypoxic or in shock.
  • If apnoeic or inadequate effort: bag-valve-mask (BVM) with appropriate mask size and two-person technique if possible.
  • Consider:
    • Nebulised salbutamol ± ipratropium for wheeze.
    • Adrenaline neb for croup/upper airway obstruction.
    • Needle decompression if tension pneumothorax suspected.

C – Circulation

  • Check: heart rate, BP, capillary refill, pulse volume, mental state, urine output.
  • Establish IV or IO access early in shocked child.
  • Initial fluid bolus for septic/undifferentiated shock: 10–20 mL/kg isotonic crystalloid over 10–20 minutes, reassess after each bolus.
  • Cardiogenic shock: smaller boluses (5–10 mL/kg) and senior input.
  • Control haemorrhage with direct pressure, splinting, pelvic binder if needed.

D – Disability

  • Use AVPU or paediatric GCS.
  • Check pupils, blood glucose, temperature.
  • Treat hypoglycaemia urgently.
  • Consider seizures, head injury, meningitis, toxins.

E – Exposure / Everything Else

  • Fully expose to look for rash, injuries, burns, bruising.
  • Prevent hypothermia: warm blankets, warmed fluids.

Red Flags Requiring Immediate Senior Help

  • Persistently poor perfusion despite fluid resuscitation.
  • Recurrent apnoea or severe respiratory distress.
  • Altered mental status (P or U on AVPU, GCS < 9).
  • Uncontrolled seizures.
  • Suspected non-accidental injury.
