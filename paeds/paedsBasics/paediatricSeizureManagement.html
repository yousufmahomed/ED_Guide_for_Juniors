Paediatric Seizure Management

First seizure or prolonged seizure in a child is an emergency. Focus on airway, breathing, circulation, and timely administration of anti-seizure medication.

Immediate Actions

  • Protect airway, position child in lateral position if possible.
  • High-flow oxygen.
  • Check blood glucose early – treat hypoglycaemia promptly.
  • IV/IO access if seizure persists.

Drug Treatment (Status Epilepticus Pathway)

Use your local status epilepticus protocol; the following are general principles.
  • First line benzodiazepine (e.g. buccal midazolam or rectal diazepam) if seizure > 5 minutes.
  • Repeat dose after 5–10 minutes if still seizing (as per local guideline).
  • Second line: IV medications (e.g. levetiracetam, phenytoin/ fosphenytoin) in discussion with senior/paediatrics.
  • Consider intubation and ICU if seizures persist despite adequate doses.

Investigations

  • Blood glucose, U&E, calcium, magnesium.
  • Drug levels if on antiepileptics (where available).
  • Consider infection markers, meningitis/encephalitis workup if indicated.
  • Neuroimaging if trauma, focal neurology, or other red flags.

Red Flags

  • Seizure > 5 minutes or recurrent without full recovery.
  • Focal neurological findings, head trauma, suspected non-accidental injury.
  • Signs of meningitis/encephalitis: fever, neck stiffness, photophobia, altered mental state.
