Paediatric DKA Guide

Paediatric Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)

DKA is a medical emergency. Avoid rapid shifts in fluid and osmolality to reduce risk of cerebral oedema.

Diagnostic Features

  • Hyperglycaemia.
  • Metabolic acidosis (low pH, low bicarbonate).
  • Ketonuria and/or ketonaemia.
  • Signs: polyuria, polydipsia, vomiting, Kussmaul respirations, abdominal pain, dehydration.

Initial Management (ED)

  1. ABC first. Oxygen if hypoxic, treat shock if present.
  2. IV access. Take baseline bloods (U&E, glucose, venous blood gas, ketones if available).
  3. Fluid resuscitation: if shocked, give 10–20 mL/kg isotonic crystalloid and reassess.
  4. Maintenance and deficit replacement: follow local paediatric DKA protocol (typically over 48 hours).
  5. Insulin infusion: usually started 1–2 hours after initial fluids, at low dose (e.g. 0.05–0.1 units/kg/hr) per local protocol.
Avoid insulin bolus in children. Correct potassium, and monitor U&E, glucose, and neuro status closely.

Monitoring

  • Hourly: vital signs, neuro observations, glucose.
  • Frequent: fluids input/output, ketones if available.
  • Repeat blood gases and U&E as per protocol.

Red Flags – Cerebral Oedema

Warning signs:
  • Headache, irritability, behaviour change.
  • Reduced level of consciousness, seizures.
  • Bradycardia, rising blood pressure.
Treat as cerebral oedema and escalate urgently according to local guidelines.
