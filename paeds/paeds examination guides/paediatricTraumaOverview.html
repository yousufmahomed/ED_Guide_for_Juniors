Paediatric Trauma Overview

Children are not small adults. Apply ATLS principles with special consideration for paediatric anatomy and physiology.

Primary Survey (ABCDE)

  • A: Airway with cervical spine protection.
  • B: Breathing – recognise pneumothorax, flail chest, pulmonary contusion.
  • C: Circulation – children compensate longer; hypotension is a late sign.
  • D: Disability – AVPU/GCS, pupils, blood glucose.
  • E: Exposure – full undressing with temperature control.

Common Injuries

  • Head injury – large head, thin skull, risk of intracranial bleed.
  • Abdominal trauma – handlebar injuries, seatbelt signs, solid organ injury.
  • Long bone fractures, non-accidental injury patterns.

Investigations

  • Plain X-rays as indicated (C-spine, chest, pelvis, extremities).
  • FAST scan where available.
  • CT imaging for significant head injury or multi-trauma as per local protocol.

Red Flags

  • Hypotension or poor perfusion.
  • Persistent vomiting, severe headache, altered consciousness.
  • Suspicion of non-accidental injury.
