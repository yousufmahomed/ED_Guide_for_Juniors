Paediatric Burns Guide

Paediatric Burns

Burns in children require careful assessment of depth and total body surface area (TBSA), prompt analgesia, and fluid resuscitation for larger burns.

Estimating Burn Size (TBSA)

  • Use age-adjusted Lund & Browder chart or local tool.
  • Exclude simple erythema from TBSA calculations.

Fluid Resuscitation (If Indicated)

Common formula (e.g. Parkland):
4 mL × weight (kg) × % TBSA
Half in first 8 hours, rest over next 16 hours.
Always follow local burns protocol.

Initial Management

  • Stop the burning process; remove hot clothing and jewellery.
  • Cool the burn with cool (not ice) running water for 20 minutes if within 3 hours of injury.
  • Cover with clean, non-adherent dressing or cling film.
  • Provide strong analgesia early.
  • Assess for inhalation injury (facial burns, singed nasal hairs, hoarseness).

Red Flags

  • Burns > 10% TBSA in children.
  • Facial, perineal, or circumferential limb burns.
  • Suspected inhalation injury.
  • Suspected non-accidental injury.
These need specialist burns referral and senior involvement.
