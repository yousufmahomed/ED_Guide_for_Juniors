Paediatric Sepsis & Shock

Early recognition and aggressive initial management saves lives. Think sepsis in any unwell child with fever, tachycardia, poor perfusion, or altered mental state.

Recognising Sepsis

  • Fever or hypothermia.
  • Tachycardia and tachypnoea (age-adjusted).
  • Prolonged capillary refill, cool peripheries, weak pulses.
  • Altered mental state: irritability, lethargy, confusion.
  • Reduced oral intake, oliguria or no urine.

Initial Management (First Hour)

  1. ABC resuscitation – airway, breathing, circulation.
  2. High-flow oxygen for hypoxia or shock.
  3. IV/IO access – send bloods (FBC, U&E, glucose, lactate, cultures if possible).
  4. Fluid resuscitation: 10–20 mL/kg isotonic crystalloid over 10–20 minutes, reassess after each bolus.
  5. Antibiotics: broad spectrum as per local protocol – give within 1 hour of suspicion of sepsis.
  6. Glucose: check and treat hypoglycaemia promptly.
Consider early involvement of senior/ICU/paediatrics for any child requiring repeated fluid boluses, inotropes, or respiratory support.

Reassessment

  • Heart rate, BP, capillary refill, mental state, urine output.
  • Watch for fluid overload: rising respiratory rate, crackles, hepatomegaly.
  • If persistent shock after 40–60 mL/kg fluid, consider inotropes and intensive care support.

Red Flags

  • Persistent hypotension despite fluids.
  • Rapidly spreading purpuric rash (suspected meningococcaemia).
  • Recurrent apnoea, severe respiratory distress.
  • GCS < 12 or P/U on AVPU.
